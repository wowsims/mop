Fusion-Fire Core is the best cleave trinket in the bed — and the rotation only needed changing once Whirlwind lost its cap

Three p5 fixtures identical in all sixteen slots except trinket one, run across twelve target counts and eight fight lengths. Fusion-Fire Core costs 5.4 % on a single target, crosses over at two, peaks +14.8 % at six — and then declines, because its cleave saturates at exactly six targets while the rest of the rotation keeps scaling. Whirlwind also turned out to be carrying an AoE cap it should never have had; removing it reopened the rotation above twenty targets and paid +7.3 % on the repo's own multi-target cases.

Engine change

Whirlwind lost its AoE cap — and it was invisible to every existing test

Whirlwind was carrying SpellFlagAoE on both its main-hand and off-hand spells, so the encounter cap min(20/targets, 1) applied to it. It should not have been — Whirlwind is uncapped. Both flags are now gone (sim/warrior/whirlwind.go). That flag has exactly one consumer in the engine (sim/core/spell_result.go:240), so removing it lifts the cap and changes nothing else.

Nothing below 20 targets moved at all

The cap multiplier is min(20/20, 1) — exactly 1.0 — for every target count up to 20, so removing the flag is arithmetically inert there. Re-measured across three trinket beds and five target counts: max |Δ| = 0.0004 DPS, which is CSV rounding. The cap only ever bit above twenty targets.

Which is why no test caught it

sim/core/test_utils.go:120 builds 21 targets for LongMultiTarget but marks index 10 DisabledAtStart — so the suite's widest case runs 20 active targets, exactly where the cap equals 1. The cap never bound anywhere in the committed suite. Arms and Protection pass untouched too (Protection has no Whirlwind; the Arms rotation does not cast it).

Re-optimisation

Six rotation decisions re-tested; two of them flip once Whirlwind is uncapped

Whirlwind's damage now grows without limit in target count, so every gate that limits Whirlwind casts became suspect. Four of the committed choices survive unchanged even at 30 targets. Two reverse — and both are crossover-shaped, so neither can be applied flat.

Mechanism

The cleave is a flat multiplier on total damage — which is why it changes no priorities

sim/common/mop/trinkets_phase_4_54.go:645. Every landed hit that deals damage — direct or periodic — rolls a fixed chance; on a proc, the hit's own damage is copied to min(5, activeTargets−1) other targets. There is no internal cooldown and no proc-mask restriction. So the expected cleave damage is p × min(5, targets−1) × total damage dealt: proportional to the total, and blind to how that total is distributed across spells.

The control that proves it

If the cleave favoured big hits, front-loading Storm Bolt and Raging Blow ahead of Bloodthirst inside the Colossus Smash window would hurt less on Fusion-Fire Core than elsewhere. Removing the AoE: Multi Target gate so it also applies on one target costs −1.167 % on Fusion-Fire Core, −1.803 % on Galakras and −1.077 % on Skeer's — the same wrong answer everywhere. No big-hit bias exists.

Why the tooltip's 0.01 % is not the number

The in-game text reads “0.01 % chance to Cleave”. The implementation scales it: GetItemEffectScalingStatValue(itemID, 0.0786, state) / 10000. At this item level that lands near 4 % per hit, which is what the fit recovers independently from damage totals alone.

The second axis

Fight length matters, but only through Galakras

Sweeping duration as well as target count turns the comparison into a surface. It is not smooth: ridges run along the duration axis. They are not noise, and they are not Fusion-Fire Core — measured against Skeer's the same surface is flat to about a percentage point. A cooldown-reduction trinket is paid in whole extra casts, so its value steps with fight length; a proportional cleave does not.

Special optimisations

Six candidates, and none of them ship

Fusion-Fire Core's Strength proc is visible to the APL: an 85 s internal cooldown clears the minIcdSeconds: 54 filter on the existing anyTrinketStatProcsActive gates, which Galakras (no stat proc) never satisfies. So there are real hooks to tune. Almost none of them pay. 20 000 iterations per arm puts 2σ near 0.07 %; anything inside that band is reported as noise, not as a small win.

Drop the trinket-proc gate on the execute-phase Raging Blow — the only one with a real effect

That line carries not(anyTrinketStatProcsActive(Str, minIcd ≥ 54s)) and not(anyStatBuffCooldownsActive(Str, Crit)), so it stands down whenever a Strength proc or Recklessness is up. Removing both terms is worth +0.17 % at three targets and +0.26 % at six on Fusion-Fire Core (5.7σ and 7.3σ), and about a third of that on the other two beds. It is −0.059 % at one target (−2.5σ), so it would need a multi-target gate. Above six targets it is exactly nothing (+0.002 % at twenty, 0.1σ): once Whirlwind runs ungated the execute-phase Raging Blow barely fires, so its own gate stops mattering. A narrow 3–6 target effect. Not shipped — one gear set is not enough to install on.

The Galakras ladder

Five versions, three outcomes — and Warforged is worth nothing at 2/2

Evil Eye of Galakras exists in five obtainable item-level versions (sim/common/mop/trinkets_phase_4_54.go:151). It carries no static stats and no gem sockets — verified against the item database — so swapping versions leaves every gem, enchant and reforge on the character untouched. Only two things scale: the readiness cooldown-reduction coefficient and the size of the Strength proc.

Warforged stops mattering once you finish upgrading

Warforged 2/2 and Normal 2/2 both land on ilvl 567; Heroic Warforged 2/2 and Heroic 2/2 both on 580. Run as separate beds they come out bit-identical — max |Δ| = 0.000000 DPS across ten target counts on both talent builds, 40 paired comparisons. Warforged only buys you a head start at 0/2 and 1/2.

Even the worst Galakras still beats Fusion-Fire Core on one target

LFR 2/2 (ilvl 542) does 487 611 on a single target against Fusion-Fire Core's 485 185 — still ahead by +0.50 %, despite 38 item levels. Below two targets the cleave is switched off entirely, and readiness beats a bigger Strength proc. From two targets up, Fusion-Fire Core beats every Galakras version.

Talent row 4

Dragon Roar wins at two targets and nowhere else

The same grid re-run on 133333 (Dragon Roar) against 133133 (Bladestorm). One APL covers both: the Bladestorm block folds away at parse time on a Dragon Roar build and the Dragon Roar line folds away on a Bladestorm build, so neither talent is handicapped by the rotation.

Why the repo's notes file disagrees

FURY_APL_OPTIMISATION.md has Dragon Roar ahead by 5.76 % at twenty targets. That was measured before Dragon Roar was given SpellFlagAoE. It carries it now (sim/warrior/talents.go:104), so the encounter AoE cap min(20/activeTargets, 1) applies — the note's own measurement showed it was doing 1.93× too much at twenty targets. Bladestorm has never carried the flag (talents.go:165) and is still exempt. Once the cap landed, the talent verdict inverted. The recommendation table in that file is stale and should be re-cut from this grid.

The two-target win is real but narrow

+1.37 % on Heroic Galakras, +1.25 % on LFR, +0.74 % on Fusion-Fire Core, +0.24 % on Skeer's — all far outside the 0.10 % noise band. At two targets Bladestorm's cap exemption is worth nothing (there is almost nothing to cleave into) while Dragon Roar's always-crit single GCD still lands in full. By three targets Bladestorm is back ahead.

Method

Notes and limits

  • All three fixtures had their embedded rotation replaced with the committed APL before running. They were saved at different times and do not carry the same list, so comparing them as-is would have measured the rotation rather than the trinket.
  • Duration variation was set to 0 for the whole grid. The fixtures ship durationVariation: 60, which would smear the very quantisation steps this sweep is trying to resolve.
  • The 288-point target × duration grid runs on 133133 — Bladestorm and Storm Bolt. The Galakras ladder and the talent comparison run on both 133133 and 133333, at 300 s only.
  • The Galakras version swap replaces one ItemSpec and nothing else. Because the trinket has no static stats and no sockets, hit and expertise totals are unchanged, so no version needed a reforge — the comparison is the trinket effect in isolation.
  • Installed in the repo: the SpellFlagAoE removal on both Whirlwind spells, and the two gated rotation lines. TestFury.results is left stale on purpose — 16 multi-target cases move and regenerating it is not this note's call to make.
  • Not installed: every Fusion-Fire Core candidate below. Those run on these three fixtures and one gear set, which is not enough to install an APL change on — a single-fixture tuning in this spec has previously measured +1.34 % on one bed and negative on three others.
  • All sweeps ran from temporary harnesses in the spec package, which have been deleted.
