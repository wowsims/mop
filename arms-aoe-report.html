Arms only plays its AoE rotation if you happen to be wearing one specific trinket

The multi-target Bloodbath → Bladestorm sequence is gated on auraIsKnown(145990) — the readiness aura that only Evil Eye of Galakras grants. On any other trinket the gate folds to false at parse time, the fallback branch can never start, and Bladestorm is cast once per fight instead of five times. Removing one condition is worth +5.9 % at two targets and +16.1 % at twenty, and exactly 0.000 % where the trinket is equipped.

the mechanism

Four branches, and only one of them can ever run

Priority line [11] is numberTargets > 1 → group "AoE BladestUwU". The group holds five branches. A strictSequence is only ready when its first action is ready and every spell in it is off cooldown (apl_actions_sequences.go:156), which turns out to disqualify three of them:

Branch [1] is the fallback that exists precisely so the sequence does not have to wait for Synapse Springs — but somebody gated it on the aura of the trinket they happened to be testing with. auraIsKnown resolves at parse time, so on Skeer's or Fusion-Fire Core the whole branch is pruned before the fight starts.

Branches [2] and [3] are dead for a different and more interesting reason. castAllStatBuffCooldowns(CritRating) collects its spell list in a post-finalize hook, after every manually-cast cooldown has been removed from the major cooldown manager — and this character casts all of them manually. With an empty list IsReady returns false forever, and because a strictSequence tests subactions[0].IsReady first, neither branch can ever begin. The parse log says so out loud: "CastAllBuffCooldownsFor(CritRating) will not cast any spells". They are also gated on a crit on-use trinket, which none of the three builds has.

That leaves the opener. Measured on the Skeer's build at 20 targets, baseline Bladestorm is 1.2 casts a fight and still 6.95 % of all damage — the single clearest sign that the line was never firing.

group "AoE BladestUwU" [1] — strictSequence[ Bloodbath, Bladestorm ] if
  auraIsKnown(145990) and
  ( ($ExecutePhase and $PotionActive) or !$ExecutePhase or !$CanPot ) and
  !$Trinket: HasCritOnUse
rage at scale

Above five targets the off-GCD rage belongs to Cleave, not Heroic Strike

Both are off-GCD 30-rage dumps. Heroic Strike hits one target; Cleave hits everything. The shipped list only lets Cleave fire at 100+ rage and only outside the Colossus Smash window, so it is cast 0.7 times per fight at six targets — effectively never. Handing it Heroic Strike's rage from a target threshold up is a large win, and the threshold matters a lot:

Two smaller rage findings, both clean wins on all three builds at every multi-target count:

  • Reserve 30 rage for Sweeping Strikes. Sweeping Strikes is a 30-rage, 10-second, off-GCD cooldown with a 10-second duration — 100 % uptime is available, but the baseline only manages 86–89 % because Heroic Strike spends the rage first. Requiring 80 rage for Heroic Strike while Sweeping Strikes is off cooldown lifts uptime to 90.9 % and is worth +0.06 to +0.21 %. Zero at one target by construction.
  • Stop casting Execute from six targets up. Execute is 30 rage for one target while Slam and Thunder Clap are waiting: +0.07 % at six, +0.21 % at ten, +0.39 % at twenty. At four targets it is a loss (−0.06 to −0.16 %), so the gate is 6, not 4.
cooldowns at scale

Heroic Leap on cooldown — but only between four and nine targets

Heroic Leap carries SpellFlagAoE and is off-GCD, so it is nearly free damage; the shipped list restricts it to the Colossus Smash window. Letting it go on cooldown from four targets up is worth +0.37 to +1.38 % — but ungated it goes −0.11 % on Galakras at twenty targets, where the encounter AoE cap (min(20/targets, 1)) has eaten the extra casts and the rotation would rather have the rage and the proc rolls. Capping the gate below ten targets keeps the gain and drops the loss.

Thunder Clap, by contrast, is already right. The shipped list casts it unconditionally from six targets; lowering that threshold costs −3.9 to −4.5 % at two targets, −0.9 to −1.2 % at three, and is only a wash at five (−0.08 % on Galakras against +0.37 / +0.58 % on the other two). Left alone.

the stack

Five edits, all target-gated, nothing lost at one target

Stacked in the order they were proven. Every one is gated so that a single target sees no change at all — verified as 0.000 %, not "within noise":

  1. Delete auraIsKnown(145990) from the AoE Bloodbath → Bladestorm branch.
  2. Heroic Leap on cooldown at 4–9 targets.
  3. Cleave takes the off-GCD rage from 5 targets up; Heroic Strike is gated below 5.
  4. Heroic Strike needs 80 rage while Sweeping Strikes is off cooldown, above 1 target.
  5. No Execute from 6 targets up.

The single-target pass's two winners are not in this stack. They were +0.13…0.19 % on all three p5 fixtures at 100 000 iterations, and gating them to numberTargets == 1 made them exactly 0.000 % above one target — but on the repo's own committed gear sets at 60 000 iterations they are −0.08 % / −0.12 % / −0.25 % on three of four beds:

structural pass

The variables were free; the group fold found a core bug

A third pass, meant to change nothing at all: name the repeated sub-expressions. The Colossus Smash debuff id 86346 appeared 45 times in the file, and one Thunder Clap sub-expression — "at least two of the three Deep Wounds are worth refreshing" — appeared twice as a 978-character byte-identical subtree.

Eight new valueVariables (11 → 19) take 4 573 characters of duplication out and drop the file 7.8 %. Substitution was by exact canonical-JSON match, which is what makes it safe: the two Last CS GCD variables use the unreacted auraIsActive(86346) — no includeReactionTime — so they have a different canonical form and were untouched. Retuning a CS-window threshold is now a one-place edit.

The group fold looked equally free and was not. Lines [9] Sweeping Strikes and [10] Thunder Clap both carried numberTargets > 1, and a group reference is ready when condition && any(inner ready) and executes the first ready inner action — so {C, group[(c1,a1),(c2,a2)]} is logically identical to the two inlined lines. Same parse validations, one line shorter. But:

The castSpell branch calls spell.CanCast(…) — deliberately not CanCastOrQueue, so a spell merely queued inside the spell-queue window cannot prematurely interrupt a channel. The generic fallback calls APLActionCastSpell.IsReady, which is CanCastOrQueue plus an MCD/GCD gate. Folding a cast into a group silently upgraded it from "castable now" to "castable or queueable".

Fixed in sim/core/apl.go: the per-action logic is now a helper returning a tri-state, and a group reference recurses into its group's actions in order under the same rules. A strict sequence deliberately still uses its own readiness — unlike a group, a sequence's members are not independently castable — and the recursion carries a depth cap. With the fix the same fold is bit-identical across all 33 cases, so folding consecutive castSpell lines into a group is now genuinely behaviour-neutral.

The fold is still not in the shipped APL — it buys one priority line, and the shipped file was already proven and suite-checked without it. It is now a safe option for a future pass. The core fix itself changes no committed rotation: 35 of 36 ./sim/... packages pass, and the one failing package is sim/warrior/arms with the same eight rows and byte-identical DPS values as before the fix. Fury, which already keeps Bladestorm inside a group, is unaffected.

notes and limits

What still has to happen before any of this ships

  • The suite agrees. TestArms reports exactly eight DPS differences out of 2 203 assertions, and all eight are the LongMultiTarget (21-target) cases: +1.50 / +2.84 / +3.16 / +3.70 % on p5 gear and +16.00 / +17.30 / +21.53 / +24.86 % on p4. Every single-target case, every AllItems case and Average-Default are byte-identical — the change is inert on one target across the whole suite, not merely within noise.
  • One gear set, one talent row (113132: Bladestorm + Bloodbath). Storm Bolt and Dragon Roar builds are untested here and the AoE group behaves differently for them.
  • The gate fix is the only change large enough to matter in play. The other four are 0.1–3.8 % and every one of them is threshold-sensitive — moving Cleave's threshold from 6 to 2 turns +2.0 % into −3.8 %.
  • TestArms.results is now stale by design on those eight cases. Regenerate with make update-tests when you are happy with the change; I did not touch it.
  • No fixture or .results file was regenerated. Editing the committed arms.apl.json will make TestArms fail on DPS diff by design.
