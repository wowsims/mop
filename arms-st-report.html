The Arms single-target rotation is already at a local optimum

Thirty-five candidate edits, three trinket builds, 100 000 iterations per arm. The largest honest gain is +0.08 % to +0.14 % — and even that one turned out to be overfitted to this bed: on the repo's own gear sets it loses on three of four. Nothing single-target shipped. The shipped priority list is tighter than it looks, and three of its oddest-looking lines are load-bearing.

the bed

One character, one trinket slot changed

Everything below runs on p5-arms-fixture.test.json: Orc, Xal'atoh two-hander, all sixteen items at UpgradeStepTwo, talents 113132 (Bladestorm + Bloodbath), Glyphs of Bull Rush / Unending Rage / Death from Above, Engineering + Blacksmithing, 300 s ±60 s single target, 100 ms reaction time. The fixture's embedded rotation is byte-identical to the committed ui/warrior/arms/apls/arms.apl.json, so there is only one baseline to argue about.

Two new fixtures were derived from it by swapping one item — trinket slot 2, Evil Eye of Galakras (105491) — for Skeer's Bloodsoaked Talisman (105632) and Fusion-Fire Core (105459). Thok's Tail Tip stays in slot 1 in all three. Because the builds differ by exactly one line of JSON, the absolute DPS numbers are directly comparable.

where each trinket wins

Galakras only pulls ahead once there is more than one target

On a single target the readiness trinket is the worst of the three: its 1 %-per-stack cooldown recovery buys extra Bloodbath and Bladestorm casts that a single target barely wants, while Skeer's stacking crit rides the Colossus Smash window. From two targets up the ordering inverts and never comes back — and Fusion-Fire Core's cleave only catches up around six.

anatomy

What the rotation actually does in 300 seconds

Before changing anything: a 3 000-iteration instrumented run on the Galakras build, one target. The rotation is GCD-saturated — about 218 globals in 300 s, roughly one every 1.37 s — and rage-balanced, spending 4 407 of the 4 480 rage it generates and wasting 1.1 % to the cap. That combination is what kills most candidate edits before they start: there is no spare global and no spare rage to redirect.

the tuning window

Thirty-five edits, six that beat the noise floor

Each candidate is one edit, measured alone, on all three fixtures. The screening pass ran 30 000 iterations (2σ ≈ 0.053 %); everything within reach of the threshold was re-run at 100 000 iterations (2σ ≈ 0.030 %). A candidate is only called a win if it clears 2σ on all three builds.

The one that does is the Heroic Strike rage floor. Outside the Colossus Smash window the shipped list demands 100 rage before dumping into Heroic Strike; 65–70 rage is better on every build (+0.04…0.07 % alone). Stacking it with Overpower-during-Recklessness — the other marginal positive, which is only proven on two builds by itself — gives +0.08 to +0.14 %, above 2σ on all three. The 65-rage variant has the higher floor (+0.094 % worst build), the 70-rage variant the higher ceiling (+0.144 % on Fusion-Fire Core). Pushing further loses: 55 rage is negative, and tightening the in-window floor to 45 helps Galakras and hurts Skeer's.

combo-hs70 re-ran hs-base-rage70 under a different sweep and returned the same three numbers to three decimals — the seeds and iteration counts are matched, so the ladder below is reproducible, not resampled.

dead lines

Three lines never fire on this build — and two of them must stay

The parse validations are the same on all three fixtures. With Bladestorm and Bloodbath talented (113132), Dragon Roar (118000) and Storm Bolt (107570) are unknown, so priority lines 7 and 19 are pruned at parse time and line 17's !spellIsKnown(118000) folds to true. Those lines are live for other talent rows, so they are not deletable — the Fury pass learned that removing a "dead" fallback cost 3.5–5.4 %.

Genuinely inert on this build: castAllStatBuffCooldowns(Strength) and castAllStatBuffCooldowns(CritRating) both report "will not cast any spells", because every stat-buff cooldown this character owns is cast manually elsewhere in the list. That matters more than it looks: the action returns IsReady == false forever rather than being pruned, so a strictSequence that starts with one can never begin. Two branches of the AoE Bladestorm group are built exactly that way — see the multi-target pass.

Also absent on this gear and therefore folded to false: 58356 and 58384 (Thunder Clap / Slam glyphs), 138126/138127, 138759, 123142, and item 81268.

verdict

The best candidate was overfitted, and nothing shipped

Everything above is measured on the three p5 fixtures — one gear set, one talent row. The Fury pass's hardest lesson was that this is not enough, so the winning stack was re-measured on the repo's own committed gear sets at 60 000 iterations (2σ ≈ ±0.034 %), with the edits restricted to numberTargets == 1 so nothing else could interfere:

Four wins, three losses, all past 2σ. A +0.1 % edit that flips sign with the buff set is not a rotation improvement, it is a fit to one bed — so the single-target list was left exactly as it was. The multi-target pass is where the real change is: one deleted condition worth up to +25 % at twenty-one targets, and provably 0.000 % here.

notes and limits

What this does not prove

  • All numbers in the ladder are one gear set and one talent row — which is exactly why the winner did not survive the cross-bed check above.
  • A +0.06 % change is real at 100 000 iterations and irrelevant in play. The honest headline is that the single-target list needs no change — and the cross-bed check turned "needs no change" from a shrug into a result.
  • durationVariation: 60 is on, so per-iteration variance is higher than a fixed 300 s fight would give. Same seed and iteration count on both arms of every comparison.
  • No fixture or .results file was regenerated.
